VERDICT: MAJOR

SUMMARY
This companion study attaches an LLM/VLM policy adapter to an existing head-on-corridor negotiation benchmark and evaluates ten (nominally) foundation models across centralized (mediator) and decentralized (agents) modes, text/image/egocentric-image inputs, and two cost modes (free vs. measured inference latency charged as frozen-robot decision time). Headline claims: reasoning models beat the forced-yielder reference via better refuge choice (-1.0 to -1.7 s); disabling chain-of-thought collapses them below the rulebook; charging real latency inverts the ranking (+103 s, some deadlocks); decentralized agents fail by model-specific disagreement modes; and a visible fair tiebreak eliminates disagreement while destroying deliberation. The results largely reproduce in the released `rows.jsonl` data — but the draft has a real internal contradiction on success/fallback, a headline latency number resting on one n=50 run of one model with an unexplained serial/concurrent confound, a figure-generation script that mislabels a zero-cost arm as "measured," and several numeric claims that do not match the released data.

MAJOR CONCERNS

1. **Direct internal contradiction: "all runs succeed" vs. deadlocks.** Sec. III-A states "A rulebook decides every encounter that the LLM does not resolve, so all runs succeed." Sec. V-C and the data contradict this: `qwen3-30b-onm` `llm_med_raw` shows success 64% with 88% fallback — the rulebook decided yet a third of encounters still deadlocked; `qwen3.8-27b-offm` shows success 82-94%. Presumably the deadlock timer fires during the measured decision freeze before/while the fallback decision executes, but the deadlock criterion and simulation horizon are never defined anywhere in the paper. Resolve the contradiction, define the deadlock/timeout rule, and state whether fallback rescues a decision that arrives after deadlock.

2. **The deployed-cost claim rests on one model, n=50, with an unexplained serial/concurrent confound.** The +103.1 s headline comes solely from `qwen3-30b-onm` (25 encounters/layout = 50 total, not the 200 encounters the text repeatedly implies). There is no thinking-on+measured arm for qwen3.8 at all, and `nemotron-3-super-onm` is 100% endpoint errors. Worse, the data reveal a concurrency artifact the paper never discusses: `qwen3-30b-offm` (non-serial) reports mean "think" of 22.0-29.7 s for the same model that answers in 0.7 s serially (`offm-s`) — i.e., measured latency under concurrent episode execution is dominated by server queueing, not inference. The paper silently substitutes "a serial rerun" for one arm without explaining that two measured regimes exist or which produced the onm numbers. If `onm` was run concurrently, +103 s is partly a benchmarking artifact (a single deployed mediator call would not queue behind 200 parallel episodes). State the execution model (serial vs. concurrent calls, whether peer robots freeze), report serial thinking-on measured latency, and give n for every number in Sec. V-C.

3. **Figure/data-pipeline integrity: Fig. 3 mislabels arms.** `make_figures.py` (lines 80-85) builds the "measured" group for qwen3.8 from `qwen3.8-27b-on0` — a think=0, *zero-cost* arm — because no `qwen3.8-27b-onm` exists, and builds the "quality only" group from `-off0` (thinking-*off*) arms while the text and caption compare thinking-on quality vs. measured. If `fig/latency.pdf` was generated by this script, it displays a -1.3 s uncharged bar inside the "measured" group and shows the wrong quality baseline; if it was hand-edited, the released script does not reproduce the paper figure. Either way, regenerate with correct arms, label n per bar, and mark missing cells explicitly rather than substituting a different condition.

4. **Sample-size asymmetry contradicts the "same seed draw / paired" claim.** Sec. III-E and the abstract claim every arm runs the same 200 paired encounters. In fact all thinking-on cells (`*-on0`, `*-onm`, nemotron-3-super cells) ran only 50 encounters (25/layout), the 1.5B smoke ran 20, and the baselines in those runs differ (rules 3.7 vs 2.9 s, fixed-priority 10.3 vs 10.7 s), indicating a different encounter population. Fig. 2's caption discloses "on cells n=50" but the text claims comparisons are "on identical encounters" — true only on the overlapping subset. State explicitly which encounters are shared, and ideally compute on/off differences on the paired subset only.

5. **Tiebreak paragraph numbers do not match the released data (Sec. V-F).** "Conflicts now resolve by chance rather than optimally: 45-51% right on tiebroken encounters, 11-14 s excess, versus the rulebook's 99% and 2-5 s." (a) No rulebook arm reaches 99% right-yielder — `rules_default` is 72%/2.9 s and the fallback-rescued `llm_agents_img` arms are 62-75%/2.5-10.6 s; only `cost_bid` is 99%. The 99% appears to be cost bidding's number misattributed. (b) The "11-14 s" hidden-draw range omits gemma4-silent (7.8 s) and vl-12b-silent (10.0 s). (c) Visible-draw right-yielder is stated as 48-58% but observed values span 45-59% (vl-12b `llm_agents_nonce` 45%, gemma4 `img_nonce` 59%). Correct each number and name the population each refers to.

6. **Reproducibility claim is stronger than the data support.** Sec. III-B claims deterministic caching makes "runs reproducible." But the same `llm_agents_img` arm gives different answer distributions across runs of the identical model/prompt: omni disagreement counts 167 vs 173 vs 169 (ego/silent/nonce runs), qwen3.8 87/84/80, gemma4 91/93/84. Caching makes runs resumable and identical *given* the cache; it does not make the model deterministic across sessions (vLLM continuous-batching nondeterminism). Either soften the claim or report cross-run variance — the "identical distributions" claim in Sec. V-F (omni 170/27/3, which I verified is exactly matched *within* the silent run) is only guaranteed within a cached session.

7. **No statistical treatment of paired comparisons.** The design's selling point is paired seeds, yet the text reports bare means; no paired-difference CIs or tests appear anywhere (per-arm 95% CIs exist only in the study reports). Several headline comparisons are small relative to per-arm CIs — e.g., omni image-vs-text 14.2 vs 16.4 s — and "beats on every model" should be backed by a paired test on per-encounter differences. Report paired CIs at minimum for the modality and thinking on/off claims.

8. **Completeness for T-ASE.** The manuscript is ~4 pages with no results table, no prompt text, no JSON schema, no deadlock definition, no statement of whether ego/nonce/silent arms ran thinking on or off (rows show empty `think` fields but this is undocumented), and no description of whether the peer robot freezes during decision time. The prompt itself is the method for an LLM-policy paper — it must appear (appendix is fine). A compact results table with n, excess+-CI, right-yielder, and fallback composition per arm is needed; currently all numbers must be reverse-engineered from prose.

MINOR COMMENTS

- Abstract vs. Sec. V-A: "92-94%" vs "92-93%". Data support 92-93% at n=200; 94% appears only in n=50 on-cells.
- Table I lists Muse-Glimmer ("experimental") but no results directory exists for it and the text never mentions it; "ten models" is nine with data plus an unlisted 1.5B smoke model. Remove the row or report its outcome (it appears only in the hosted endpoint's model list).
- The 1.5B "smoke test" (Sec. V-A) is n=20 on a different serving stack (GGUF on :8080), and the quoted "25-47 s" mixes mediator and agents arms across two different runs (one of which charged measured latency). Flag n and the different stack, or move to a footnote.
- Sec. V-D: "text+image is worse than image alone on three of four" — the released data show worse on all four (gemma4 imgtext 6.4 vs img 3.9).
- Sec. V-D: "All image arms still sit far below reasoning-on text" — you mean far *above* in excess delay / worse; rephrase.
- Sec. V-E: ego-image-beats-text is quoted only for vl-12b and qwen3.8; omni (3.4 vs 3.0 s) and gemma4 (3.8 vs 2.8 s) go the other direction. Report all four.
- Sec. V-E also shows info level matters: gemma4 `agents_plans` disagrees only 21% (vs 46% img), while omni/vl-12b disagree ~97-98% on plans. The "disagreement is structural" story survives, but the plans arms complicate it — worth one sentence.
- Degenerate thinking-on raw cells: `qwen3.8-27b-on0` `llm_med_raw` is 98% error fallback (excess = rulebook's 3.7 s); `qwen3-30b-on0` raw is 80% parse fallback. Fig. 2 will display these as respectable bars; annotate fallback composition or omit.
- Abstract: "+11 to +30 s, below the hand-written rulebook" reads ambiguously (below = worse than?); say "worse than."
- Sec. V-C: raw-arm measured excess is +301.5 s over successful runs only (36% deadlock excluded) — survivorship bias if ever reported; you currently report only the 64% success, which is fine, but note the exclusion rule.
- `qwen3.8-27b-offm` had `server_models: unavailable (ConnectionResetError)` with 64-84% error fallback and per-layout asymmetry (~100 s single_bay vs ~1.7 s two_bays) — flag this corrupted run in the released data.
- "Unspoofable" draw: a deterministic hash of broadcast data is trivially computable by both agents — which is exactly why the visible arm collapses. Substantiate "fair" with the empirical card-win split; the Blum 1983 citation is heavier machinery than the implemented scheme uses.
- "Held-out seeds" (Sec. III-A) — held out from what? Give the seed range per arm.
- Latency "30-130 s" in Sec. V-C: observed *means* are 31.1 and 100.3 s; state whether 130 is a max.
- "Wrong-yielder agreements... 13.7-38.0 s": unverifiable from released aggregates (gemma4's wrong-agreed rows show 3.0-12.6 s); give per-model values or a table.
- "Code and per-episode results are released" — no URL/artifact reference.
- Related work is thin for T-ASE: refs.bib already contains directly relevant uncited entries (`dias2006market`, `gerkey2002sold`, `amir2015mapfauction`, `gautier2022negotiated`, `chen2024scalable`, `alghamdi2025opinion`); position against auction-based coordination and LLM-multirobot work.
- Companion-paper dependence: `paper1` is an anonymous companion submission; the benchmark, baselines, and Gazebo-transfer claims all rest on it. The paper gives enough to stand alone in prose, but expect reviewers to push on unverifiable baseline details.
- NVFP4 quantization: add one limitations sentence that absolute quality may differ at full precision.
- Leftover comment line 101 ("% cost_bid wins deployed.") — remove.

QUESTIONS FOR THE AUTHORS

1. Was `qwen3-30b-onm` run serially or concurrently? What fraction of the 31.1 s mean think is server queueing vs. model inference? Does serial thinking-on measured data exist for any model?
2. What is the deadlock criterion/horizon, and does the fallback rulebook decision still execute (but too late) on deadlocked measured-latency runs?
3. Does `rules_default` receive the same per-refuge predicted-delay numbers ("plans") that LLM prompts carry? If not, is part of the screen advantage an information asymmetry rather than deliberation?
4. Why are all thinking-on cells n=50? Are their seeds a strict prefix/subset of the n=200 draw?
5. What happened to Muse-Glimmer — hosted endpoint failure?
6. What is the empirical card-win split of the "fair" draw over the 200 encounters?
7. Do both robots freeze during measured decision time, or only the caller? In agents mode, is the charged time the slower of the two calls (as Sec. III-B says) even when the other robot also waits?
8. Per-model values for "right-yielder on agreed encounters (58-71%)" and "wrong-yielder agreements (13.7-38.0 s)"?
