SUMMARY
This letter benchmarks decentralized negotiation policies for head-on robot encounters in a single-lane corridor with pull-in bays, running identical agent code in a 2D simulator and in ROS 2/Gazebo with TurtleBot3 Waffles. Eight policy families are compared against a best-forced-yielder reference across geometry, asymmetry, bid noise, misreporting, fairness, packet loss, convoys, a T-junction, map inconsistency, blocked bays, and explore/hold modes. This Round-3 revision fills every earlier `[XX]` placeholder (prediction-error MAE, paired CIs, mismatch trace) and adds a causal account of the T-junction 2D<->Gazebo gap. Where I could recompute from the released data, the new numbers hold up exactly (see below); the remaining issues are one incompletely-supported causal claim, newly load-bearing but undocumented protocol parameters, stale hedges that now contradict the filled-in text, and artifact-completeness gaps.

MAJOR CONCERNS

1. **The new T-junction causal claim rests on protocol machinery that is never specified, and it explains only the exit variant.** Sec. IV-H (line 172) now attributes the gap to "whether the branch robot leaves the stem before or after A clears the bay," invoking a "60 s proceed timeout" and a pending-yield release that appears nowhere in Sec. III-B. Reading `agent.py`, the relevant undocumented parameters are: `proceed_timeout = 60.0` (releases a dual-proceed wait — it *sets* the 196.8 s outlier's delay), `park_timeout ~ U[6,12]` s (when a parked yielder may leave absent a fresh peer STATE — directly governs the "release timing" the paragraph attributes to perception/communication), `state_timeout = 1.0` s (STATE staleness: at the 2-8 s latencies in the extreme-comms grid every received STATE is already stale, silently disabling the release/contradiction checks — this materially bears on the Sec. IV-C claims), `neg_period = 0.3` s, and a hard-coded 10 s renegotiation backoff (`reactive_until`, line 592). For a letter whose contribution 1 is an open, reproducible benchmark — and whose Round-1 reproducibility concern I marked resolved — these are the parameters the new causal story actually depends on. Resolution: extend the Sec. III-B safeguard/timing description by a few sentences (they are already in the released code).

2. **The explanation covers only the exit variant; the turn-in variant contributes the larger share of Delta_D and the stated figure for it is wrong.** Decomposing the -16.8 s by variant from `gazebo_main_runs.csv`: the exit (`tj`) rows sum to -260 s (cb -264.7, fp +18.6, rd -14.0) — the part the trace explains. The turn-in (`tjgoal`) rows sum to -345 s, i.e., a mean -19.2 s per run, with per-run weighted-delay gaps of -10 to -29 s (e.g., fp s2: 56.7 vs 85.3 s). The text dismisses this with "Gazebo makespans were 1-5 s shorter," which (a) reports makespan while Delta_D is weighted total delay — the measured makespan differences are 0.5-6.4 s, not 1-5 s, and the delay gaps are 3-7x larger; and (b) is left unconnected to the timing-release mechanism, although the data are consistent with the same ~20 s systematic release offset appearing in both variants (which would actually *strengthen* the story if stated). Resolution: decompose the T-junction row's Delta_D by variant, correct the makespan range, and either extend the release-timing account to the turn-in runs or say plainly that portion remains unexplained. The "we do not claim that T-junction delays transfer" conclusion itself remains supported and should stand.
